# Face-match accuracy

How well does the face check separate the right person from the wrong one? This page is generated by
`backend/tools/accuracy_report.py` from real photos taken during phone testing, and shows the sample size
next to every number. **The sample is small**, so treat it as evidence about behaviour, not as a precise
accuracy percentage (see "Limits" below).

## How it is measured

Every registered reference photo is compared with every check-in selfie, exactly like a real check-in:
cosine distance between the two face embeddings (lower = more alike). A pair is *genuine* if both photos
show the same person and *impostor* if not. The ground truth is a labels file filled in by hand.

In this run: 2 people. **P1** is the project author (reference photo plus check-ins accepted for their own
USN, and one check-in made against P2's registration). **P2** is P1's brother, registered as a different
student. So every impostor pair here is a *sibling* pair, which is the hardest realistic case. Strangers are
easier to reject: in earlier phone tests a different person scored 0.43 to 0.59 on Facenet512.

How the app uses the number (see `backend/face_settings.py`):

| Distance | What happens |
|---|---|
| below 85% of the pass mark | counted present straight away |
| from 85% of the pass mark up to the pass mark | **held**: the faculty confirms or rejects it |
| above the pass mark | rejected (the student can retry) |

## Reading the results

* **Facenet512 separates people better than VGG-Face** on this data: its genuine and impostor ranges
  overlap only slightly (genuine 0.19-0.27, sibling 0.26-0.37), while VGG-Face's overlap heavily
  (genuine 0.36-0.50, sibling 0.42-0.50). That is why Facenet512 is the default.
* **Siblings can land in the grey zone.** One sibling pair scored 0.258, just under the 0.30 pass mark. No
  single threshold separates it from genuine matches (genuine matches reach 0.27), which is why matches in
  that zone are held for the faculty instead of being counted automatically.
* **A stricter threshold is not a fix.** Dropping the pass mark to 0.25 would stop the sibling here but would
  also reject genuine check-ins (1 of 6 here).

## Limits (be honest about these)

* Only **two people and 12 pairs**. The rule of three says that "0 wrong in 6" only proves the true rate is
  probably below about 50%. A defensible number needs roughly 20+ people with a few photos each.
* The 6 genuine pairs are not independent (one person, similar rooms and lighting).
* Genuine labels for the check-in selfies come from the author's own testing, not from independent
  verification.
* No liveness test: a photo of a face held to the camera is a different problem from face matching and is
  not measured here.

## Making the numbers better

1. Have classmates register and check in during a real session. Faculty Confirm / Reject decisions then
   give you honest labels.
2. List the stored photos: `python tools/accuracy_report.py --template tools/labels.csv`, fill in the
   `person` column (labels like P1, P2; never names), then:
   `python tools/accuracy_report.py --labels tools/labels.csv --models Facenet512,VGG-Face --out report.md`
3. Keep the photos and the labels file out of version control (they are biometric data); only this report,
   which contains numbers and person labels, is meant to be shared.
